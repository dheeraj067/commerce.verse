Choosing your stream

Why Not Commerce?

Many students ask why they should not pick Commerce after Class 10. Here are real reasons to consider it.

🎓 Many study paths

After Class 12 Commerce you can study B.Com, BBA, Economics, Law, Hotel Management, Design or Digital Media, and prepare for CA, CS and CMA. Entrance rules vary, so check each course.

💼 Many career fields

Commerce connects to accounting, banking, insurance, taxation, marketing, finance, business management, e-commerce and entrepreneurship.

💰 Skills you use in life

You learn about profit and loss, budgeting, loans, interest, GST and how businesses work. These help in a job, a family business or your own money decisions.

🚀 A base for starting a business

Accounts, business studies and economics teach costs, pricing, customers and records, which every entrepreneur needs.

💻 Fits the digital world

Online selling, digital payments and digital marketing all use commerce ideas. Learning them alongside Commerce can open modern career paths.

🧩 Flexible subjects

Many boards let you add Mathematics, Informatics Practices or Computer Science with Commerce. Check your school’s options.

Honest advice
No stream guarantees a job or a high salary. Commerce suits you best if you enjoy numbers, business and organised work. Talk to teachers, parents and people already working in the field before you decide.
Financial wisdom
Revenue and profit are not the same thing. A business can earn large revenue and still make a loss.

By activity: trading businesses buy and sell goods (a grocery shop). Manufacturing businesses turn raw material into products (a biscuit factory). Service businesses sell skills or help (a tuition centre, a bank, a salon). Some businesses do more than one of these.

By size: India groups businesses as micro, small and medium (MSME) using investment and turnover limits. Smaller businesses often have simpler rules for registration, tax and filing.

Who Pays Tax and Who Does Not (India)

What is tax? Tax is a compulsory payment to the government. It funds roads, schools, hospitals and defence. Two common ones for businesses are income tax (on earnings and profit) and GST (on selling goods and services, collected from the customer and passed to the government).

Who has to pay income tax

  • Individuals whose taxable income is above the exemption limit. Higher income is taxed at a higher rate, so people who earn more pay a larger share.
  • Businesses and professionals that make a profit, such as shopkeepers, doctors, freelancers and traders.
  • Companies. Every company pays tax on its profit. There is no basic exemption limit for companies.
  • Partnership firms and LLPs, at a flat 30% on profit (plus surcharge and cess where they apply).
  • Residents are taxed on income earned anywhere in the world. Non-residents are taxed on income earned or received in India.

Who does not have to pay income tax

  • A resident individual whose taxable income is up to ₹4 lakh in the new regime, which is the default.
  • Because of the Section 87A rebate, a resident individual with taxable income up to ₹12 lakh pays no tax under the new regime on normal income (this rebate does not cover income taxed at special rates, such as some capital gains). A salaried person also gets a ₹75,000 standard deduction.
  • In the old regime the limit is ₹2.5 lakh. It is ₹3 lakh for people aged 60 to 80 and ₹5 lakh for people above 80.
  • Agricultural income is exempt from income tax in most cases.
  • Registered charitable trusts can be exempt when they follow the conditions of the law.
  • Some receipts are exempt, such as gifts from specified relatives and scholarships.
  • Someone with no income, such as a student with no earnings, has no tax to pay. A person with income below the limit may still file a return, for example to claim a refund.

GST: who must register and who need not

  • A business that sells only goods must register once yearly turnover crosses ₹40 lakh in most states. The limit is ₹20 lakh in some states and union territories.
  • A business that sells services (or goods and services together) must register above ₹20 lakh, or ₹10 lakh in some special category states.
  • Smaller businesses below these limits need not register. Some cases need registration even below the limit, for example certain e-commerce sellers.
  • Small traders can choose the composition scheme, with a lower flat tax and simpler returns, up to a turnover limit.
  • Some essentials are exempt from GST, such as fresh vegetables and fruit, and school education.
Remember
Tax is based on income and turnover, not on the size of the building or the number of staff. Tax rules and limits change with each Budget, so check incometax.gov.in, gst.gov.in or a chartered accountant before acting. These notes are for learning only and are not tax advice.
Journal Entries

A journal is the book of original entry. Every transaction is recorded in date order with its debit and credit. Rules: Personal – debit the receiver, credit the giver. Real – debit what comes in, credit what goes out. Nominal – debit expenses and losses, credit incomes and gains.

Journal trick
Find which accounts are affected first, then decide which one to debit and credit.

Trial Balance

A trial balance lists all ledger balances to test arithmetic accuracy: total debits should equal total credits. Errors like wrong amounts posted to the right side of both accounts, or omitted entries, do not stop it from agreeing, so agreement is not proof that books are correct.

Balance Sheet & Final Accounts

The balance sheet shows assets (what the business owns), liabilities (what it owes) and capital on one date. Trading account gives gross profit (sales − cost of goods sold). Profit and loss account gives net profit (gross profit + other incomes − expenses). Net profit increases capital. Formats differ between school textbooks and company law formats.

Careers

See what each career involves, the skills it needs and a simple way to begin. Choose the field that matches your interests.

💼 Commerce and Finance

Finance

Chartered Accountant (CA)

Handles audit, tax, accounts and financial reporting for businesses.

Key skills: Accounting, tax law, analysis, ethics.

How to start: Register with ICAI after Class 12 and move through Foundation, Intermediate and Final, with practical training (articleship).

Compliance

Company Secretary (CS)

Makes sure a company follows company law and corporate rules.

Key skills: Company law, drafting, attention to detail.

How to start: Join ICSI after Class 12 and clear its stages with training.

Costing

Cost and Management Accountant (CMA)

Works on costing, budgeting and cost control so a business can price and plan well.

Key skills: Costing, Excel, analysis.

How to start: Register with ICMAI and clear its levels.

Banking

Banking and Insurance

Works in banks, insurance firms and loan or customer teams.

Key skills: Customer handling, basic accounts, numbers.

How to start: Usually after graduation, through recruitment exams such as IBPS and SBI. Check the notice for each year.

Tax

Tax and GST Consultant

Files returns and advises people and businesses on income tax and GST.

Key skills: Tax rules, accounting software, communication.

How to start: B.Com or M.Com plus short courses in tax and GST, then practise under a professional.

Markets

Financial Analyst

Studies companies, markets and investments to guide money decisions.

Key skills: Excel, valuation, reading financial statements.

How to start: Degree in commerce, finance or business, with certifications such as NISM or CFA.

Accounts

Accountant and Bookkeeper

Records daily transactions, prepares statements and handles payroll and GST entries.

Key skills: Tally, Excel, accuracy.

How to start: B.Com or a certificate course in accounting software, often as a first job.

📣 Digital Marketing

Marketing

SEO Specialist

Helps websites appear higher in search results.

Key skills: Keyword research, writing, Google Search Console.

How to start: Build a small website or blog, learn the free Google tools and show your results.

Social

Social Media Manager

Plans posts, grows audiences and answers followers for brands.

Key skills: Content ideas, design basics, analytics.

How to start: Run a page for a local shop and keep a record of growth.

Content

Content Writer

Writes articles, scripts and website text.

Key skills: Clear writing, research, editing.

How to start: Publish samples on a blog and send them to small businesses.

Ads

Performance Marketer

Runs paid ads and tracks cost against sales.

Key skills: Ad platforms, numbers, testing.

How to start: Start with a small ad budget for a real shop and report the results.

Email

Email and CRM Marketer

Builds email lists and sends useful offers to customers who agreed to receive them.

Key skills: Writing, segmentation, reports.

How to start: Learn a free email tool and run a small campaign.

Data

Digital Analyst

Reads website and campaign data to show what works.

Key skills: Google Analytics, Excel, charts.

How to start: Practise on free sample data and present one clear finding.

🚀 Business and Enterprise

Business

Entrepreneur

Starts and runs a business, from a small shop to an online brand.

Key skills: Planning, selling, money management.

How to start: Use the Start Your Own Business page to see steps, capital and costs for your idea.

Teaching

Teacher or Lecturer

Teaches Accountancy, Business Studies or Economics.

Key skills: Subject knowledge, clear explanation.

How to start: Degree and B.Ed for school; M.Com or MBA and UGC NET for college. Check current rules.

People

HR and Operations Manager

Looks after hiring, staff matters, supply and day-to-day running of a business.

Key skills: Communication, planning, problem solving.

How to start: BBA or B.Com, then MBA or a specialised course.

Sales

Sales and E-commerce Manager

Grows sales through shops, marketplaces and online channels.

Key skills: Selling, customer insight, data.

How to start: Start with a sales or marketplace role and learn how listings, ads and orders work.

🧭 How to choose and begin

  • Pick the subjects you enjoy most: numbers, writing, people or technology.
  • Learn one useful tool early, such as Excel or Tally.
  • Do a small real project, for example keep a shop’s accounts or run a page for a local business.
  • Talk to a person who works in the field and ask what a normal day looks like.
  • Try an internship or part-time work as soon as you are eligible.

Types of companies in commerce

🛍️ Retail and trading

Buy goods and sell them to customers. Profit comes from the margin between buying and selling price.

📦 E-commerce

Sell online through websites and apps. Payments, delivery and customer reviews matter most.

🏦 Banking and finance

Take deposits, give loans and help payments. Trust and regulation are central.

🛡️ Insurance

Protect people and businesses from risk in return for a premium.

🚚 Transport and logistics

Move goods between sellers and buyers. They remove the hindrance of place.

🏭 Manufacturing

Turn raw material into finished goods. Costing and inventory are key.

💻 IT and digital services

Build software and online tools for other businesses and customers.

🤝 Cooperatives

Owned by members who join together, such as farmers pooling their milk.
